16.1: Why It Matters- Grammar Basics
Why learn to examine proper English grammar?
There are several different types of English. While there are some obvious examples of different varieties (e.g., American and British English), there are other differing types, such as formal vs. informal English or verbal vs. written English. There are also different varieties of English that are unique to cultural, societal, or professional groups.
While all of these types of English are equally dynamic and complex, each variety is appropriate in different situations.  If you’re sending a quick casual message—via social media or texting—you don’t need to worry much about capitalization or strict punctuation. Sure, use five exclamation points standing alone, if that gets your point across.
However, there’s this thing called Standard American English. This English is used in professional and academic settings, so that people can communicate with and understand one another. While this online jargon is great for quick communication, it isn’t formal: it isn’t a part of the commonly accepted conventions that make up Standard American English.
Grammar is a set of rules and conventions that dictate how Standard American English works. These rules are simply tools that speakers of a language can use. When you learn how to use the language, you can craft your message to communicate exactly what you want to convey.

2.6: Grammar (1) الجملة الاسمية والفعلية
In this section, you will be able to:
- Learn about basic sentences in Arabic
- Learn about verbal sentences
- Learn verb agreement
Arabic sentences الجمل في اللغة العربية
There are two types of sentences in Arabic: nominal اسمية and verbal فعلية. Nominal sentences الجمل الاسمية begin with a noun or pronoun, and verbal sentences الجمل الفعلية begin with a verb. اسمية is a nisba adjective from the word "اسم" which means noun or name. فعلية is a nisba adjective from the word "فعل" which means verb.
Example for a nominal sentence: الأُسْتاذ جَديدٌ
Example for a verbal sentence: يَدْرُسُ الطالِبُ الحُقوقَ
Parts of Nominal Sentence أجزاء الجملة الاسمية
Nominal sentence الجملة الاسمية starts with a noun or pronoun, and it has two parts a subject المبتدأ and a predicate الخبر. The nominal sentence does not need the verb to "be" like in English, and it is implied. The predicate can be a noun, adjective, adverb, or verb. In this section, we will focus on the function of the verb agreement with the subject in both gender and number. If the subject is singular, the verb will be singular. If the subject is dual, the verb will be dual. If the subject is plural, the verb will be plural.
Examples singular subjects: الطّالِبُ يَدْرُسُ - الطَالِبَة تَدْرُسُ
Examples dual subjects: الطّالِبان يَدْرُسان - الطَالِبتان تَدْرُسان
Examples plural subjects: الطّلابُ يَدْرُسون - الطالِباتُ يَدْرُسنَ

Parts of Verbal Sentence أجزاء الجملة الفعلية
Verbal sentence الجملة الفعلية starts with a verb and is followed by the subject of the verb, or it is implied within the verb if it is not mentioned explicitly. The subject comes after the verb; this is considered a very elegant style in the Arabic language and can't happen in English. In verbal sentences, the verb agrees with the subject in gender and not in number. If the subject is dual or plural, the verb will still be singular. If the subject is feminine, the feminine markers should appear at the beginning and the end of the verb.
